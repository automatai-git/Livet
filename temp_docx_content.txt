1
Book BOS → HNL flight (June 27)
Book by April 25 — June is peak-priced
No direct BOS→HNL flights until Dec 2026 — Delta's nonstop is suspended. All routings are 1-stop via LAX, SFO, SEA, or DEN. Total transit ~13–16 hours including layover. June is historically the most expensive month on this route.
Compare on Google Flights, Kiwi, Skyscanner. Best 1-stop options: United (via SFO/DEN), American (via LAX/PHX), Delta (via LAX/SEA). Aim for SFO or LAX layovers — most frequent backup options if delayed. Book a same-day connection (no overnight). Budget USD 700–1,100 per person one-way at this point.
2
Book Hawaii → Norway return flight
Book at the same time as task #1
Returning from HNL/LIH (Lihue) via West Coast or Asia. Routings vary widely in cost and time. Booking the return alone (vs. BOS+return as one ticket) gives you flexibility on which island you finish on.
Two main options: (a) HNL → mainland → Europe → BGO (United/Lufthansa, SAS, KLM) — typically 22–26 hrs. (b) HNL → Tokyo/Seoul → Europe → BGO — longer but sometimes cheaper. If finishing on Kauai, fly LIH → HNL → onward (cheap interisland connector). Lock in the date of departure as the day after Kauai ends.
3
Book inter-island flights (HNL ↔ KOA, HNL ↔ LIH)
Book by May 1 for best fares
Two interisland legs needed: Boston → Kona (start), Kona → Honolulu (mid-trip), Honolulu → Lihue (end). Hawaiian Airlines and Southwest dominate. Fares jump from ~USD 70 to USD 200+ as the date approaches. Schedule changes are common — book direct with the airline.
Book all three legs together if possible. Hawaiian Airlines website is the most reliable. Allow minimum 2.5 hrs between mainland arrival and interisland connection at HNL. Pre-book one checked bag per person to avoid airport fees. Note: KOA (Kona) is on the Big Island's west side — your starting base.
4
Book Manta Ray Night Snorkel (Kona)
Book by mid-May (4–6 weeks ahead)
This is your highest-rated experience on the entire trip (10/10). Top operators (Sea Quest, Anelakai, Fair Wind Hula Kai, Kona Snorkel Trips) cap small-group boats at 12–17 people. Premium operators sell out 3–6 weeks ahead in summer. Free re-ride if no mantas, so book for your FIRST night in Kona to leave a backup window.
Two routes — pick one: (a) Premium small-group: Anelakai Adventures (canoe, 6 max, USD 149) or Sea Quest (Keauhou Bay, RHIB). (b) Mid-tier: Big Island Manta Ray Night Snorkel via Viator (USD 99–130). Book your first or second night in Kona. Bring a GoPro — board lights are bright enough; no external flash needed.
5
Book Big Island accommodation (5 nights)
Book by mid-May
Split: 3 nights Kona (west, dry, beach + manta) + 2 nights Hilo or Volcano Village (east, rainforest + Volcanoes NP). Volcano Village has limited inventory — only ~30 rentals/B&Bs. Kona resorts fill up earliest in summer. The split-base approach saves ~5 hrs of driving back and forth.
Kona: Royal Kona Resort, Outrigger Kona Resort, or Airbnb in Kailua-Kona town (USD 220–350/night). Volcano side: Volcano Village Lodge, Volcano Inn, or Kilauea Lodge (USD 200–280/night). Avoid resorts on the Hilo side — too far from the park; stay within 15 min of Volcanoes NP entrance for night-glow visits.
6
Reserve Haena State Park parking + entry (Kauai)
Opens 30 days ahead at 12:00 AM HST — sells out in MINUTES
This is the SINGLE most competitive booking of the trip. Required to access the Kalalau Trail / Hanakapiai Falls hike. Park caps at 900 visitors/day, with only 70 parking stalls for non-residents. Summer slots sell out within minutes of opening. There is no walk-up option.
On the day exactly 30 days before your hike date, log into gohaena.com a few minutes before midnight HST (10:00 AM Bergen time the next day). Have payment ready. If sold out: shuttle reservations are easier to get — book the Kauai North Shore Shuttle from Waipa Park & Ride (free parking). Backup: refresh gohaena.com between 7–8 AM HST daily for cancellation drops.
7
Book Na Pali Coast boat or raft tour (Kauai)
Book by mid-May
Rated 10/10 in your guide and effectively unmissable on Kauai. Summer (June/July) is the only reliable season — winter swells often cancel tours. Top operators (Captain Andy's, Holo Holo, Na Pali Riders, Kauai Sea Tours) sell out 4–6 weeks ahead in peak season.
Choose your style: Catamaran (smoother, food/drinks included, bigger group, USD 180–230) — Holo Holo Charters or Captain Andy's. Zodiac raft (faster, gets into sea caves, more adventure, no shade, USD 160–200) — Na Pali Riders or Kauai Sea Tours. Morning departures = calmer seas + better visibility. Bring seasickness meds — Hawaiian channel waters can be choppy.
8
Book Oahu accommodation (3 nights)
Book by mid-May
Waikiki is dense — 30,000+ rooms — so availability is rarely the issue, but PRICE escalates fast in June. Mid-range Waikiki hotels jump from USD 180 to USD 350+ within 4 weeks of arrival. Staying in Waikiki keeps you walkable to the surf school AND the morning shark dive shuttle pickup point.
Recommended zone: Waikiki Beach (south end is quieter, north end has nightlife). Solid mid-range: Outrigger Reef, Aston Waikiki Beach Hotel, OHANA Waikiki East (USD 250–380). Splurge: Halekulani, Royal Hawaiian. Avoid downtown Honolulu — far from beach and from your morning activities.
9
Reserve rental car for Big Island (5 days)
Book by early May
Big Island is 4,000 sq mi — you cannot do this trip without a car. Demand spikes in summer; rates can double in 2–4 weeks. Discover Cars and Costco Travel often beat direct booking by 20–30%.
Pick up at Kona (KOA), drop off at Hilo (ITO) — saves the 2.5-hr return drive on your last day. Compact SUV recommended (small SUV = USD 50–80/day in summer). 4WD only needed if doing self-drive Mauna Kea summit (otherwise skip).
10
Reserve rental car for Kauai (4 days)
Book by early May
Kauai has only one main road (Kuhio Highway loops part of the island). No road circles the whole island — it deadends at Haena (north) and Polihale (west). Without a car you cannot reach the Na Pali boat dock, Waimea Canyon, or Wailua. Bus service is minimal.
Pick up and drop off at Lihue (LIH). Small SUV ~USD 65–95/day in summer. Same booking strategy: Discover Cars, Costco Travel, or DiscountHawaiiCarRental.com. Skip rental car for Oahu — rideshare and shuttles cover everything in Waikiki + North Shore.
11
Confirm passport validity + ESTA / visa status
Verify NOW
You're already in the US for the World Cup, but worth confirming: Norwegian passport must be valid 6 months past return date (mid-July). ESTA expires 2 years from issue date — confirm yours covers the full trip. Travel insurance gaps are common when extending US trips with a Hawaii leg.
Check passport expiry: must be valid through ~January 2027 minimum. Verify ESTA at esta.cbp.dhs.gov. Confirm Bank Norwegian / Nordea Premium travel insurance covers Hawaii (it does — it's US territory) and check if there's a max trip length (most Norwegian cards = 90 days).

12
Book Helicopter Volcano Tour (Big Island)
Book by late May (3–4 weeks ahead)
Rated 9/10 in your guide. Best operators (Blue Hawaiian, Paradise Helicopters, Sunshine Helicopters) sell out 3–4 weeks ahead in summer. Doors-off flights add USD 100–200 but are dramatically better for photography. Whether or not Kilauea is actively erupting on your date, the helicopter tour also covers Hamakua coast waterfalls and rainforest.
From Hilo (50–60 min, USD 230–380): closer to volcano, cheaper. From Waikoloa (2-hr Big Island Spectacular, USD 500–650): also sees Kohala coast + waterfalls. Recommended: book a Hilo-side flight on day 4 or 5 of the Big Island leg. Morning flights have best visibility. Flexibility tip: book a 'weather guarantee' tour — many operators offer free reschedule.
13
Book Cage-Free Shark Dive (Oahu North Shore)
Book by late May
Bucket-list item: open water with Galapagos and sandbar sharks, no cage. Top operators (One Ocean Diving, Pelagic Life, Deep Blue) cap at 6–10 people per boat. Summer is peak demand and best conditions (calmer seas, better visibility).
One Ocean Diving (USD 150) is the most respected — research-affiliated, smaller groups. Pelagic Life and Deep Blue are good alternatives. Morning slots (7 AM, 9 AM) = best visibility. If you partner is unsure, the cage option (North Shore Shark Adventures, USD 130) provides a barrier and 100% sighting guarantee. Combine with a North Shore exploration day — Haleiwa shrimp trucks for lunch (Giovanni's, Romy's).
14
Book Waikiki surf lesson (Oahu)
Book by late May
Birthplace of modern surfing, summer south swells = consistent gentle waves, 95%+ stand-up rate even for first-timers. This is on your bucket list (#26 in Underwater & Marine: 'Learn to surf — stand up'). Top schools (Hans Hedemann, Faith Surf School, Moku Hawaii) are heavily booked summer mornings.
Two options: (a) Group lesson USD 90–129/pp, 2 hours. (b) Private USD 175–225/pp, 1.5 hrs. For genuine 'check the box' value, do a private — guaranteed instructor attention. Book a 7 AM or 8 AM slot for calmer water. After the lesson, rent a board (USD 25–35/hr) and practice for an hour solo — this is when it actually clicks.
15
Book Mauna Kea Summit / Stargazing (Big Island)
Book by late May (or self-drive)
Rated 8/10 in your guide. 13,796 ft — one of the world's premier stargazing locations. Two options: (a) Self-drive to summit (FREE, but requires 4WD and altitude tolerance). (b) Guided tour (USD 200–250) including telescopes, hot drinks, parka. Tours sell out 3–4 weeks ahead in summer.
Recommended: Hawaii Forest & Trail or Mauna Kea Summit Adventures. Pickup from Waikoloa or Kona. Acclimate at Visitor Information Station (9,200 ft) for 30 min before continuing — altitude sickness is real. Don't fly above sea level for 24 hrs after a scuba dive (relevant if you've done the manta dive).
16
Plan Volcanoes NP visit + monitor eruption status
Plan now, monitor weekly from May
Kilauea has been erupting episodically since Dec 2024 — Episode 44 was April 9, 2026 (8.5 hrs of 800-ft fountains). Episodes have lasted 1 day to 8 days, separated by pauses of 1–21 days. There's a real chance you hit an active episode in late June, but not guaranteed. Even between episodes, the crater glow at night is mesmerizing.
Bookmark and monitor weekly: usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/volcano-updates. NPS park status: nps.gov/havo. Plan TWO Volcanoes NP visits if possible: one daytime (Crater Rim Drive, Thurston Lava Tube, Steam Vents) and one after dark (crater glow, peak experience). Park is open 24 hours. Bring a mask for vog (volcanic gas). Park entry: USD 30/vehicle, valid 7 days.
17
Book Kealakekua Bay snorkel tour (Big Island)
Book by late May
Rated 8/10 — the best snorkeling on the Big Island. Captain Cook monument area, crystal water, abundant marine life, spinner dolphins often present, sea turtles common. Pairs naturally with a chill recovery day after the manta dive.
Three access modes: (a) Boat snorkel cruise — Fair Wind catamaran (Hula Kai or Fair Wind II), USD 130–170, includes BBQ lunch. (b) Guided kayak + snorkel — Adventures in Paradise, USD 100–120, more active. (c) Steep hike + DIY snorkel — free but demanding, needs early start. Recommended: Fair Wind morning cruise — easiest, best gear, often sees dolphins on the way out.
18
Book Kauai accommodation (4 nights)
Book by late May
Two viable bases: Poipu (south shore, sunnier, closer to Waimea Canyon and Na Pali boat dock at Port Allen) or Princeville/Hanalei (north shore, lusher, closer to Haena/Kalalau trailhead). For a 4-night trip with both north and south activities, Poipu wins on weather + central location.
Poipu: Koa Kea (boutique, USD 380–550), Sheraton Kauai (resort, USD 320–450), or Poipu Beach Athletic Club (condo, USD 250–350). Princeville: 1 Hotel Hanalei Bay (luxury, USD 700+), Westin Princeville (USD 400–600). Best value for your trip: Poipu condo for 4 nights, plan the Kalalau hike day as an early-morning drive (1.5 hrs to Haena).
19
Book Mauna Kea or Kilauea night photography opportunity
Book by late May (if doing photo-led tour)
Stacks well with your photography interest. Both Mauna Kea (stars) and Kilauea (crater glow at night) offer once-in-a-lifetime astrophotography or night-landscape opportunities you cannot replicate in Norway.
Mauna Kea: book a stargazing tour with telescopes, but also bring your own camera + tripod (Sony, Canon, anything with manual mode). Settings: ISO 1600–3200, f/2.8 if possible, 15–25 sec exposure. Kilauea night glow: shoot from Kīlauea Overlook or Uēkahuna (check current closures). Self-drive — no tour needed; just arrive after sunset.

Book Wailua River kayak (Kauai)
Book 2–3 weeks ahead
Hawaii's only navigable river — paddle to Uluwehi (Secret) Falls + jungle hike + swimming hole. ~5 mi RT paddling. Good change of pace from strenuous Kalalau hike or boat tour. Rated 7/10 in guide.
Operators: Wailua Kayak & Canoe (rental USD 60), Outfitters Kauai (guided USD 110). Recommended: rental for self-paced exploration, but guided is easier first time. Half-day, easy to fit into a chill day.
21
Plan Waimea Canyon + Koke'e State Park day
Logistics — no booking needed
'Grand Canyon of the Pacific' — 14 mi long, 3,600 ft deep, multiple lookouts and hikes (Cliff Trail, Canyon Trail, Pihea Trail in Koke'e). Free to drive in. Note: Koke'e camping closed for construction May 2026 onward, but day use unaffected.
Drive Waimea Canyon Drive (Hwy 550) early morning — afternoon clouds often roll in. Stop at: Waimea Canyon Lookout, Pu'u Hinahina Lookout, then continue to Koke'e for Kalalau Lookout (best Na Pali view from above). Bring layers — Koke'e is at 4,000 ft and 10–15°F cooler than Poipu. Pack lunch — limited food options up the mountain.
22
Plan Diamond Head sunrise hike (Oahu)
Reservation opens 14 days ahead — book day-of arrival
Iconic short hike, 1.5 hrs RT, panoramic Waikiki views. Reservation system in place since 2022 — non-residents must reserve. USD 5/person + USD 10 vehicle. Sunrise slots fill 7–10 days ahead in summer.
Reserve at gostateparks.hawaii.gov (Diamond Head). First entry 6:00 AM — book this slot. Sunrise is around 5:50 AM in late June, so you'll catch the post-sunrise glow at the top. 30 min up, 30 min down. Bring water, headlamp for early start, sturdy shoes (volcanic rock is loose).
23
Book Hanauma Bay snorkel (Oahu) — OPTIONAL
Reservation opens 2 days ahead — book at 7 AM HST
Marine preserve, calm shallow snorkel, abundant fish. Rated 7/10 in guide — pleasant but NOT as strong as Kealakekua (Big Island) or anything on Maui. Skip if your snorkel itinerary already feels full.
If interested: book at pros.hnl.info exactly 2 days in advance at 7 AM HST. USD 25/person + USD 3 parking. ALTERNATIVE: spend the morning at Waikiki for surf practice instead — better use of the same time block.
24
Book at least ONE high-end dinner (Hawaiian regional cuisine)
Book 2–3 weeks ahead for top spots
Aligns with your Food & Drink bucket list. Hawaii Regional Cuisine (Roy Yamaguchi, Alan Wong, Peter Merriman pioneers) is a genuine cultural experience — uses local fish, tropical fruit, native ingredients. Worth one signature dinner per island.
Big Island: Merriman's Waimea (farm-to-table, founder of HRC). Oahu: MW Restaurant (Wade Ueoka, modern HRC) or Senia (Nationally acclaimed). Kauai: Bar Acuda (Hanalei tapas) or The Beach House (Poipu, sunset view). Reservations via OpenTable or restaurant website.
25
Plan North Shore Oahu day (post-shark dive)
No booking — just plan logistics
Pairs with shark dive morning. Haleiwa town, surf beaches, shrimp trucks, Sunset Beach, Banzai Pipeline (calm in summer). Dole Plantation (skip — overrated). Local feel is the polar opposite of Waikiki's tourist energy.
Itinerary: Shark dive 7 AM. Lunch at Giovanni's Shrimp Truck or Romy's Kahuku (USD 14–18). Drive to Sunset Beach + Pipeline (calm in summer = swimmable). Optional: Waimea Bay for cliff jumping (small jump, free). Coffee/sunset at Hale'iwa Beach. Drive back to Waikiki by 7 PM.
26
Pre-purchase America the Beautiful National Parks pass
Buy before trip — saves money on entry
Volcanoes NP (Big Island) is USD 30/vehicle for 7 days. The annual pass is USD 80 and pays off if you'll visit any other US national park within a year (good for 12 months from purchase). Doesn't cover Haleakala sunrise reservation fee or Haena State Park (state, not federal).
Buy online: store.usgs.gov/pass. Or buy at the Volcanoes NP entrance booth on arrival. Skip if you don't anticipate other NP visits in the next year.
27
Stock up on travel-specific gear before leaving Bergen
Buy 2–3 weeks before trip
Specific items get expensive on the islands or aren't available at the right quality. Buying in Norway saves USD 50–150 vs. tourist markups in Kona/Lihue.
Pack/buy: reef-safe sunscreen (Hawaii law — non-reef-safe is BANNED, look for non-nano zinc oxide); rash guard for snorkel/surf; quick-dry hiking pants/shorts; trail shoes with grip (Kalalau is muddy); 30+L daypack; dry bag for boat trips; GoPro/waterproof case; headlamp; warm layer (Mauna Kea / Volcanoes NP at night = cold).

35
Daily: check eruption status before Volcanoes NP visit
On-trip
Eruption episodes start with little warning, peak in 8–24 hours, then end. If an episode is starting, drop other plans and get to the park.
Bookmark on phone: usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/volcano-updates and nps.gov/havo. Check at breakfast each Big Island day. Three USGS livestream cams on YouTube give real-time confirmation.
36
Daily: hydration discipline (especially Big Island)
On-trip
Hawaii is humid + sun is intense. Combined with hiking and altitude (Mauna Kea, Kilauea, Kalalau), dehydration creeps up. Headaches and fatigue blamed on jet lag are usually dehydration.
Carry a 1L reusable bottle (refill stations everywhere). Drink at least 3L/day. Add electrolyte tabs (Liquid IV, Nuun) on hike days. Skip alcohol on activity days — save it for chill evenings.
37
Sleep strategy: anchor wake-up time to local sunrise
On-trip
You arrive from EDT (Boston) — Hawaii is 6 hours behind. Going to bed at 'normal' Boston time = midnight HST. Better to lean into early mornings, which align with most peak experiences (manta dive prep, sunrise hikes, calm-water snorkeling, helicopter flights).
First 2 days: don't fight the early wake-up (6 AM HST = noon Boston time). Use it. Plan your manta dive, helicopter, or Kalalau hike for early in the trip when you're naturally awake. By Kauai you'll be fully adjusted.
38
Take photos but don't film everything
On-trip
A common mistake on once-in-a-lifetime experiences (manta ray dive, lava glow, Na Pali sunset) is spending the whole experience behind a viewfinder. The memory of being present hits harder than the footage.
Pre-set your camera/GoPro for each scenario the night before. Shoot for the first 5 minutes, then put it down. Manta dive: 90% shutter, 10% just floating. Na Pali boat: shoot the cliffs, then drop camera for the sea cave passes.
39
Build in true rest days (don't over-schedule)
On-trip
12 days is enough time to over-pack and burn out. Your guide flagged this as a known pattern. Activity-dense days need recovery days, especially after Kalalau (8 mi strenuous) and helicopter + volcano days.
Hard guideline: at least 2 days per island should have ONE planned activity max. Use those days for beach, food, exploration, or sleep. Recovery days are when partner-time happens — that's where the trip memories actually consolidate.
40
Bucket list update — log experiences as completed
On-trip + after
You're going to knock out 8–12 entries from your bucket list on this trip. Worth tracking in real time so you can mark them off in v3.0 of the document.
Likely completions: Active lava (#13 Natural Phenomena), volcanic eruption from safe distance (#14), lava tube (#15), helicopter ride (#19 Motorsport), surf stand-up (#26 Underwater), sea turtle sighting (extends Oman list), manta ray swim, US national park visits, plus possible: cliff diving (Black Rock, Maui — but you're skipping Maui), shark dive (without cage), and others. Take a photo at each as proof for the log.
